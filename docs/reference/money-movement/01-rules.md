# 01 - What the section 25F rules allow about HOW scholarship money reaches a vendor

Written 2026-11-19. Section 25F sources are local files only:
- `PR` = proposed regulations REG-117199-25, Federal Register Vol. 91 No. 190, Oct 2, 2026 (`scratchpad/sources/proposed_rule_REG-117199-25.txt`; line numbers cited as `PR Lnnnn`).
- `TR` = temporary regulations TD 10057, effective Dec 1, 2026 (`scratchpad/sources/temp_regs_TD10057.txt`).
- Already-verified notes: `docs/reference/sgo-verification-rules.md` (F80, F81, F97, F100) and `docs/reference/payments-and-rails.md` (facts 5, 15, 15A, 16).

Tags: [proposed-reg] = regulation text, not final. [preamble] = Treasury explanation, not rule text. [temp-reg] = in force from Dec 1, 2026. [web] = fetched today, URL given. [unverified, from memory] = not confirmed today. NOT IN SOURCES = searched, absent.

---

## ANSWER IN FIVE LINES

1. The proposed rules allow four payment routes: direct to the school (mandatory for school-charged tuition and fees), direct to a verified, unrelated vendor that must refund overpayments, receipt-backed reimbursement to the family, or a "qualified digital wallet" run by a third party.
2. The wallet is defined by what it does (purchase requests, tracking, controls), not by who runs it. Nothing requires the operator to be a bank. Its controls can be "pre-approving vendors and paying vendors directly **or** requiring timely submission of receipts". So a vendor the wallet never signed up can still be paid, because the receipt branch covers it.
3. The rules say nothing about cards, prepaid accounts, interchange, rebates, processor fees, money transmission or interest earned on balances held at a wallet. They neither allow nor forbid a wallet operator earning from the vendor's normal card-acceptance fee.
4. On the text, interchange that the vendor pays is not taken out of the student's award, and it is not an SGO expense. Then it does not obviously count toward the SGO's 10 percent. But no source says so, and Treasury's private-benefit language plus the required audit of payment methods mean the SGO and its auditor will see the arrangement. "The SGO doesn't know" is not an option. "The vendor doesn't need to know" is possible on the card/receipt route.
5. Getting interchange at all requires a card issued by a bank. Without its own bank deal, Passport would have to sit on top of an existing card or wallet program that already has an issuing bank and take a revenue share. Revenue per $1,000 cannot be stated: the rate and the share are both unverified.

## FACTS

**F1. Definition of "qualified digital wallet" (Q1).** [proposed-reg] High confidence.
> "(13) Qualified digital wallet. The term qualified digital wallet means an electronic payment platform in which a third-party provider provides a streamlined interface for managing the administration of section 25F scholarships by having eligible students and their families submit purchase requests, tracking approved expenses, and maintaining controls to ensure that all payments are made for qualified elementary or secondary education expenses by pre-approving vendors and paying vendors directly or requiring timely submission of receipts."

SRC: PR L4949-4956, Prop. Reg. 1.25F-1(a)(13). The preamble states the same definition at PR L396-404.

**F2. What a wallet does for the SGO, part 1: verification safe harbor.** [proposed-reg] High.
> "(v) Qualified digital wallet. An SGO will be considered to meet the requirements of this section if it uses a qualified digital wallet to pay qualified elementary or secondary education expenses."

SRC: PR L5482-5484, Prop. Reg. 1.25F-3(c)(5)(v). The preamble says this exists because stakeholders asked to verify spending "by using a third-party vendor that verifies the expenses and prevents duplication, which stakeholders referred to as a digital wallet" (PR L390-393).

**F3. What a wallet does for the SGO, part 2: the money counts as spent when it is transferred.** [preamble] High.
> "provided an SGO does not retain ownership of funds transferred to a third party for disbursement through a qualified digital wallet, the SGO will be treated as having spent the transferred funds as of the date of transfer for purposes of the 90 percent of income spending requirement."

SRC: PR L1035-1039. This appears only in the preamble. The reg text says only that amounts are "treated as spent when the amounts are paid" under the cash method (PR L5426-5429, 1.25F-3(c)(4)(iii)(B)). "Retain ownership" is not defined.

**F4. Treasury asks for comments on the wallet definition. Comments are due Dec 1, 2026.** [preamble] High.
> "The Treasury Department and the IRS request comments on this definition of qualified digital wallet and whether additional safe harbors for verification ... should be included in the final regulations."

SRC: PR L404-408. Deadline: "Written or electronic comments must be received by December 1, 2026" (PR L99-100).

**F5. General duty and the allowed payment methods (Q2).** [proposed-reg] High.
> "an SGO is required to verify that scholarship funds are used exclusively for qualified elementary or secondary education expenses in accordance with the allowable methods of payment described in paragraphs (c)(5)(ii) through (v)."

SRC: PR L5446-5452, 1.25F-3(c)(5)(i). The SGO must also have "systems to prevent and detect the duplication of scholarship awards to the same student for the same ... expense".

**F6. Route A: reimbursing the family.** [proposed-reg] High.
> "An SGO will not be treated as satisfying this paragraph (c)(5)(ii) if money is paid to the family of the eligible student, unless the payment is a qualified reimbursement, meaning a reimbursement of expenses for which the family ... provides a receipt that verifies both that the payment was made and that the expense is a qualified ... expense and, before making the reimbursement, the SGO satisfies its procedures to prevent a single expense from being reimbursed for more than the total cost by more than one source."

SRC: PR L5453-5465, 1.25F-3(c)(5)(ii). Conditions: a receipt proving both payment and qualification, plus a duplicate check before paying. No vendor obligation applies.

**F7. Route B: direct to the school. This route is mandatory for school charges.** [proposed-reg] High.
> "Scholarship funds used for payments for tuition, fees, room and board, and other similar expenses described in section 530(b)(3)(A) that are charged by the school must be paid directly to the school. The school must return any payments from the SGO that are in excess of the student's costs or that are disbursed by the SGO in error."

SRC: PR L5466-5471, 1.25F-3(c)(5)(iii). The preamble puts the duty on the SGO: "require the SGO to require the school to return" (PR L1087-1090; notes F100).

**F8. Route C: direct to another vendor.** [proposed-reg] High.
> "An SGO will be considered to meet the requirements of this section if it pays a vendor directly for expenses described in section 530(b)(3)(A) and the vendor: (A) Has been verified as an appropriate provider of such services or items; (B) Is not related, directly or indirectly, to the scholarship recipient; and (C) Is required to reimburse the SGO for any payments from the SGO that are in excess of the student's costs or that were disbursed by the SGO in error."

SRC: PR L5472-5481, 1.25F-3(c)(5)(iv).

**F9. Route D: a qualified digital wallet** (F1, F2). Under the definition, the wallet's controls are either "pre-approving vendors and paying vendors directly" or "requiring timely submission of receipts". [proposed-reg] High.

**F10. The excess-payment refund duty (Q3): who is bound, and how.** [proposed-reg] High on the text; the mechanism is NOT IN SOURCES.
- School (route B): "The school must return" excess or erroneous payments. The preamble says the SGO must "require" this.
- Other vendor (route C): the vendor "Is required to reimburse the SGO". This is a condition of using route C.
- Wallet (route D): 1.25F-3(c)(5)(v) imposes **no** refund condition on vendors. The only cross-reference is the return-of-excess accounting rule, which names only paragraphs (iii) and (iv): "An SGO that receives a return of any payments made to a school or vendor that are in excess of the student's costs, or that were disbursed in error, as set forth in paragraphs (c)(5)(iii) and (iv) ... must treat the return as income received in the year of the return" (PR L5432-5438, 1.25F-3(c)(4)(iii)(D)).
- Family (route A): there is no vendor duty. The control is the receipt plus the duplicate check.
- The sources do not say how a vendor is "required" to refund (contract, terms of payment, policy) or who verifies a vendor as an "appropriate provider". (Same finding as notes F80.)

**F11. Cards (Q4).** [proposed-reg] High. The only card reference is about donors paying the SGO: "cash" includes "electronic transfer (including, for example, by credit or debit card)" (PR L370-372, preamble; reg text at PR L4945-4948 drops the card example). TR has the same single mention (TR L183). Nothing addresses cards used to **pay vendors**.

**F12. Prepaid accounts, interchange, rebates, processor or merchant fees, money transmission, float (Q4).** NOT IN SOURCES. Searches of PR, TR and the statute file for "interchange", "rebate", "prepaid", "stored value", "float", "merchant", "processing fee", "money transmit" and "excess benefit" returned 0 hits each (run 2026-11-19). This matches payments-and-rails fact 16: "Third-party platform fees and payment-processor fees are not addressed."

**F13. "Earnings" on money waiting to be spent (Q4).** [proposed-reg] High, but only for the SGO's own account. The segregated account is "the separate account exclusively for qualified contributions and the earnings therefrom" (PR L4970-4975, 1.25F-1(a)(18); TR L1538-1541 [temp-reg]). For safe-harbor and multistate SGOs, earnings credited to that account are part of the 90 percent income base: "the total amount of qualified contributions received by, and earnings credited to, the section 25F segregated account" (PR L5405-5415). Nothing addresses interest earned on funds **after** they move to a wallet operator, or who keeps it. NOT IN SOURCES.

**F14. The 10 percent test, and what counts toward it (Q5).** [proposed-reg]/[preamble] High. The SGO must spend at least 90 percent of "income of the organization". By default that is gross receipts "unreduced by any expenses" (PR L5398-5401). For the safe harbor it is the 25F account's contributions plus earnings. "only expenditures on scholarships for eligible students are taken into account" (PR L905-907). The safe harbor exists "to allow SGOs to raise funds for administrative costs that would not count toward the 90 percent" (PR L913-916).

**F15. Private benefit (Q5).** [preamble] High that the text exists. It is context, not a 25F rule.
> "an organization is not described in section 501(c)(3) if it provides goods or services that confer more than incidental private benefit or otherwise exceed what is reasonably necessary to accomplish those purposes."

SRC: PR L1112-1115. Also: "a scholarship must be motivated by a disinterested educational purpose, not by private or business interests" (PR L1130-1132, citing Rev. Proc. 76-47). Treasury asks for comments on how 501(c)(3) interacts with 25F (PR L1133-1135).

**F16. The audit must cover how payments are made.** [proposed-reg] High. The annual audit must cover "The method(s) the organization uses to track how scholarship money for qualified elementary and secondary education expenses is paid (whether by direct payment, through a qualified digital wallet, or by reimbursement)" (PR L6057-6060, 1.25F-4(e)(3)(iv)).

**F17. Legal status.** High. The payment-route rules are only in the proposed regs. TR reserves 1.25F-1T(a)(13)-(15) (TR L1531) and has no wallet or vendor rules. Reliance: SGOs "may rely on these proposed regulations for qualified contributions made on or after January 1, 2027 ... provided that [they] follow the portions ... in their entirety and in a consistent manner" (PR L2406-2413).

**F18. Which expenses qualify is still open.** [preamble] High. Guidance on which expenses qualify "will be issued separately under section 530" (PR L1144-1147). Camps are not addressed (notes F77).

**F19. Debit interchange regulation, outside 25F.** [web] Medium (general description only, no rates captured).
> "Certain small debit card issuers, government-administered payment programs and reloadable general-use prepaid cards are exempt from the interchange fee limitations."

URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-about.htm, fetched 2026-11-19. Interchange is described there as a fee "received by a debit card issuer", which means the issuing bank. Specific rates and caps were not confirmed today.

**F20. Industry practice.** [unverified, from memory] Existing state education-savings-account wallets (for example ClassWallet-type programs) use a mix of marketplace vendors who are paid directly and restricted cards or reimbursement for vendors who are not enrolled. Card programs run by non-banks sit on an issuing bank through a program manager, and the program manager may receive a share of interchange. Not confirmed today. Do not cite.

## WHAT IT MEANS FOR PASSPORT

1. **Being a "qualified digital wallet" is the way into the money flow, and the definition does not require a bank charter or bank status** (F1). Passport's software (purchase requests, tracking approved expenses, controls) maps directly onto the definition's wording. If the SGO "does not retain ownership" of funds it transfers to the wallet, the transfer counts as spending under the 90 percent rule (F3). That is a selling point for SGOs. Actually holding and moving the funds is a separate legal question, about money transmission and custody, that these sources do not answer (F12).
2. **Vendors that never signed up.** Route C (direct pay) needs a vendor that has been "verified" and is "required to reimburse" (F8). That is hard to meet for a vendor that does not know Passport exists. The wallet's receipt branch (F1, F9) fits better: the family pays at any vendor with a wallet-controlled card, or pays and submits a receipt, and the wallet enforces timely receipts. Route D imposes no vendor refund condition in the text (F10). So the rules as proposed allow vendors to stay unaware. **They do not allow the SGO to be unaware.** The SGO picks the wallet, and the auditor must review the payment method (F16).
3. **Where a fee can come from without marking up the scholarship.** The rules are silent on all of these (F12):
   - (a) Interchange or rebate share on a card the vendor accepts. The vendor pays its normal acceptance cost, and the student's purchase price is unchanged.
   - (b) A software fee the SGO pays from administrative funds outside the 25F account (F14; payments-and-rails 15A).
   - (c) Interest on funds held at the wallet. This is unaddressed (F13), and taking it raises the "retain ownership" and private-benefit questions. Do not plan revenue on it until counsel has looked at it.
4. **Rough revenue per $1,000 paid to a vendor.**
   - Route (a): $1,000 x issuer interchange rate x Passport's share of it. Neither the rate nor any share was verified today (F19, F20), so no number is given here.
   - Routes paid by ACH or check directly to the vendor generate no interchange.
   - Tuition charged by a school must be paid "directly to the school" (F7). That is probably the largest pool of dollars, and it probably sits outside card economics unless a wallet card payment counts as "directly". That point is unsettled (see Open Questions).
5. **Without a bank of its own:** interchange is received by the issuer (F19). So route (a) means partnering with an existing card or wallet program manager that already has an issuing bank. In practice that is the bank partnership, held by someone else. Route (b) needs no bank at all and is the cleanest option under these sources.
6. **Comment window:** Treasury wants comments on the wallet definition, and they are due Dec 1, 2026 (F4). A comment asking Treasury to confirm that (i) wallet card payments satisfy "directly to the school" and (ii) the operator's revenue from vendor-side fees is not an SGO expense would turn guesswork into rule text.

## RISKS AND BLOCKERS

- **Proposed rules only** (F17). The wallet route, the receipt branch and the "spent on transfer" rule could all change in the final regulations. An SGO relying on them must follow them "in their entirety".
- **Concealment does not work.** The SGO must choose and document the payment method, and the auditor must cover it (F16). Revenue the SGO cannot see, or a cut that looks like it comes out of scholarship value, invites the private-benefit concern (F15). It could also put the SGO's 501(c)(3) status at risk, and with it SGO status (notes F97). Any interchange or revenue share should be disclosed to the SGO in writing.
- **No markup:** route B and route C vendors must refund amounts "in excess of the student's costs" (F7, F8). Any surcharge passed through to the scholarship would be a refundable excess.
- **School tuition:** "must be paid directly to the school" (F7). If a card payment through a wallet does not count, the largest flows earn no interchange.
- **Bank dependency for card revenue** (F19). Money-transmission or custody licensing for holding SGO funds is not addressed in the sources: [unverified, from memory] that state licensing may apply. Needs counsel.
- **Which expenses qualify** is unsettled (F18). A vendor network built around camps may end up with nothing to pay for.

## OPEN QUESTIONS (not answered by the sources)

1. Does paying through a wallet (by card or otherwise) satisfy the "paid directly to the school" requirement in 1.25F-3(c)(5)(iii), or does route (v) displace it?
2. What does "retain ownership" mean (F3)? Can funds sit in a wallet-held account, a for-benefit-of account or a card balance and still count as not owned by the SGO? Who owns any interest or earnings on them?
3. Under the wallet's receipt branch, must a vendor still be "verified as an appropriate provider" and "required to reimburse" excess payments? The text of (v) does not say so; the final rules might.
4. Is operator revenue from vendor-paid interchange, rebates or program-manager shares a private benefit, an SGO expense, or neither? The sources say nothing (F12, F15).
5. Who verifies a vendor as "appropriate", and how is a refund duty imposed on a vendor that has no contract (F10)?
6. Does money transmission or custody licensing apply to a non-bank wallet operator? This is outside the 25F sources.
7. Actual interchange rates, exemption status of the likely issuer, and typical program-manager revenue shares. All unverified today (F19, F20).
