# 02 — Earning on vendor payments by issuing cards, without your own bank partnership

Researched 2026-11-19. Web facts were fetched that day with plain curl; saved copies are in the session scratchpad under `research/cards/`. Section 25F text comes only from the local proposed-rule file (REG-117199-25, `sources/proposed_rule_REG-117199-25.txt`, "PR" below).
Tags: [fetched 2026-11-19] = confirmed online today · [25F proposed-reg: not final] · [inference] = my reasoning · [unverified, from memory] = not confirmed today.

## ANSWER IN FIVE LINES

1. Yes, it can work. The SGO pays each vendor with a single-use virtual card. The card-issuing provider brings its own bank, and the vendor's card processor pays interchange to that bank, out of the vendor's normal card fee. The platform gets a share. Families pay nothing and the price is not marked up.
2. Stripe Issuing for Connect fits on paper: partner banks are included, controls are good, and a virtual card costs $0.10. But Stripe's rules ban "consumer use" (payments "for personal, family, or household use"), and Stripe does not publish what share of interchange a platform keeps. You need Stripe's written approval for this use case before building.
3. Gross interchange on a $1,000 commercial prepaid card payment online is about $26.60, if the issuing bank is under $10B in assets. If the bank is a large bank like Fifth Third, it is about $0.72, and the model is dead. Platform revenue = (your share) x $26.60 - $0.10 - losses. Your share is unknown until a contract.
4. The model does not cover tuition paid by ACH or check, vendors that do not take cards, or bills over Stripe's fixed $10,000 limit per authorization. Large tuition, the biggest dollars, is exactly where card payments fail.
5. Section 25F risk: under the proposed rules a vendor paid directly must be "verified" and "required to reimburse the SGO" for overpayments. A vendor that never signed anything may not meet that. This is a question for counsel. An adult must sign every provider contract.

## FACTS

**Stripe Issuing**
1. Available in the US, with commercial cards for businesses. Stripe "partner[s] with multiple trusted banks"; you "Determine your eligibility to use Issuing by contacting Stripe sales." Source: docs.stripe.com/issuing.md and /issuing/how-issuing-works.md [fetched 2026-11-19]. So there is no self-serve go-live; approval is required.
2. Issuing banks named in Stripe's US compliance guide: Celtic Bank, Cross River Bank, Fifth Third Bank ("*Celtic Bank Users only* ... *Cross River Bank Users only* ... *Fifth Third Bank Users only*"). Source: docs.stripe.com/issuing/compliance-us.md [fetched 2026-11-19]. Which bank your program gets is not stated.
3. Size of those banks under the debit interchange cap (Regulation II), from the Federal Reserve's lists as of 2025-12-31: Celtic Bank Corporation = "Exempt", Cross River Bank = "Exempt", Fifth Third Bank N.A. = "Not Exempt". Source: federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/files/RegII_SmallIssuerExemption_Exempt.csv and ..._NonExempt.csv [fetched 2026-11-19]. Lithic/Increase-type banks checked: First Internet Bank, Sutton, Evolve, Lead, Column, Pathward, Grasshopper and Thread are all "Exempt". "Increase Bank" is not on either list (the Fed says de novo banks may be missing).
4. Connect structure: "To issue cards, each business entity must use a connected account. Issuing only supports connected accounts ... where your platform is responsible for requirements collection and loss liability, also known as a Custom connected account." Source: docs.stripe.com/issuing/connect.md [fetched 2026-11-19]. So each SGO = one connected account. The platform does KYB collection and carries losses.
5. Funding: "add funds to the Issuing balance ... This balance represents funds reserved for Issuing and is safely separated from your earnings". It can be funded from the account's Stripe balance or by top-up/ACH credit; US wire push-funding is in "Preview". Cards are prepaid against this balance. With insufficient funds "the authorization will be denied". Sources: docs.stripe.com/issuing/funding/balance.md, /issuing/controls/real-time-authorizations.md [fetched 2026-11-19].
6. Spending controls: `allowed_categories`/`blocked_categories` (MCC), merchant countries, and `spending_limits` per authorization or per time interval. Merchant-ID locking "is in private preview". Default "500 USD per day" if unset; "an unconfigurable default spending limit of 10000 USD also applies to each authorization". Source: docs.stripe.com/issuing/controls/spending-controls.md [fetched 2026-11-19]. There is no native "single-use" card type in the pages fetched. [inference] Build single-use yourself: an exact-amount limit, a real-time approval check of the merchant, then cancel the card after capture.
7. Real-time authorization: the `issuing_authorization.request` webhook; "If Stripe doesn't receive your approve or decline response within 2 seconds" a fallback setting decides. Stripe also warns that when Stripe declines but can't reach the network, "the network approves ... [funds] might still be captured." Source: /issuing/controls/real-time-authorizations.md [fetched 2026-11-19].
8. Refunds come back to the card's balance "even if your card status is inactive or canceled". Source: /issuing/purchases/transactions.md [fetched 2026-11-19].
9. Price: "$0.10 per virtual card", "$3.50 per standard, physical card", disputes "$15.00 per dispute"; "Earn a share of interchange revenue." / "Get in touch". Source: stripe.com/pricing [fetched 2026-11-19]. The URL stripe.com/issuing/pricing returned 404.
10. Revenue share: "Users who hit a certain volume threshold get a percentage of the interchange earned from all card purchases. Stripe uses gross interchange". No threshold or percentage is published. Source: stripe.com/guides/earn-revenue-by-issuing-cards [fetched 2026-11-19]. The compliance guide also says that charging connected accounts "fees beyond Stripe's fee of 0.10 USD" may require your own disclosure, approved by Stripe (compliance-us.md).
11. Restricted use, the key blocker: "Consumer use of Stripe Issuing is when an Issuing card is created to fully or partially enable payments for personal, family, or household use, including: Providing a payment method loaded with or that accesses consumer funds; Cards that disburse payroll or payouts; Any other uses that directly or indirectly enable payments using the consumer's funds." Also, platforms creating cards for customers' staff "must implement Stripe Issuing on Connect"; and accounts with 12 months of no card activity are closed. Source: stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses [fetched 2026-11-19]. Marketing and UI need "Stripe's prior express approval" (compliance-us.md).
12. Age: "Only people 13 years of age or older may open a Stripe Account ... If User or User's Representative is not 18 ... User must add a Representative who is an adult (which may be a parent or legal guardian) ... Representative agrees to be responsible and liable". Source: stripe.com/legal/ssa [fetched 2026-11-19]. [inference] For an Issuing platform contract, assume the adult parent (or the parent-owned company, through an adult officer) signs and is the account representative.

**Interchange economics**
17. Who pays: "Merchants do not pay interchange reimbursement fees; merchants pay 'merchant discount' to their financial institution ... all these services may be included in their merchant discount rate". Fees "are paid to cardholder financial institutions". Source: Visa USA Interchange Reimbursement Fees, rates effective 24 Oct 2026, usa.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/download/merchants/visa-usa-interchange-reimbursement-fees.pdf [fetched 2026-11-19]. In practice the vendor funds interchange inside its ordinary card fee. The vendor receives less than face value, and the SGO pays exactly the price.
18. Visa commercial prepaid, exempt bank (same PDF, Table F): Business Prepaid / Purchasing Prepaid "Card Not Present 2.65% + $0.10", "Card Present 2.15% + $0.10", "Non-Qualified 2.95% + $0.10", "Visa Purchasing Prepaid Large Ticket 1.45% + $35.00". The REGULATED column (large bank) is "0.05% + $0.21*" (+$0.01 fraud adjustment). Visa Purchasing (non-prepaid) "Commercial Card Not Present 2.70% + $0.10"; "Commercial Product Large Ticket 1.30% + $35.00" [fetched 2026-11-19].
19. Consumer comparison (same PDF): consumer credit "Education 1 (applies to transactions ≥ $500) 1.53% + $0.05" and "Education 2 ... 1.43% + $0.05" [fetched 2026-11-19]. Durbin cap: a covered issuer "may not receive ... an interchange fee that exceeds $0.21 plus 0.05 percent ... plus a $0.01 fraud-prevention adjustment"; banks under "$10 billion" are exempt. Fed 2024 data: Visa dual-message debit averaged 1.50% of value on exempt transactions and 0.44% ($0.22) on covered ones. Source: federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-average-interchange-fee.htm [fetched 2026-11-19].
20. Card network and bank costs are deducted before a "net" share, or ignored under a "gross" share (Stripe guide, fact 10) [fetched 2026-11-19]. Typical BaaS platform shares are often quoted in the tens of percent of interchange [unverified, from memory; do not model on this].
21. Surcharging: Visa's merchant page covers "Merchant credit surcharging" and requires notifying the acquirer 30 days ahead. Source: usa.visa.com/support/small-business/regulations-fees.html [fetched 2026-11-19]. Visa rules do not allow surcharges on debit or prepaid cards [unverified, from memory]. Vendors can still refuse cards or set card minimums and maximums.

**Licensing, KYB, 25F, fraud**
22. FinCEN excludes from "money transmitter" a person that "Provides prepaid access" or "Accepts and transmits funds only integral to the sale of goods or the provision of services, other than money transmission services". The "provider of prepaid access" is the single program participant designated as "principal conduit". Source: eCFR 31 CFR 1010.100 (version 2026-11-01) [fetched 2026-11-19]. State MTL exemptions (agent-of-payee, bank-agent) vary by state [unverified, from memory].
23. 25F, paying vendors directly: the vendor "(A) Has been verified as an appropriate provider ...; (B) Is not related ... to the scholarship recipient; and (C) Is required to reimburse the SGO for any payments ... in excess of the student's costs or ... disbursed by the SGO in error." Tuition and fees "must be paid directly to the school". No funds may go to the family unless it is "a qualified reimbursement". PR lines 5452-5481 [25F proposed-reg: not final].
24. 25F "qualified digital wallet": a third-party platform "maintaining controls ... by pre-approving vendors and paying vendors directly or requiring timely submission of receipts." PR lines 4949-4956 [25F proposed-reg: not final]. Returned excess payments count as SGO income in the year returned (PR lines 5432-5438).
25. ESA debit-card lesson (Arizona Auditor General, Report 16-107 and its 24-month follow-up dated Oct 25, 2018, azauditor.gov/sites/default/files/2023-11/16-107_Report.pdf and 16-107_24Mo_Followup.pdf [fetched 2026-11-19]): even though "the bank denies purchases at merchants with MCCs that the Department has not approved ... some parents have been able to circumvent these controls". FY2018 had "more than 900 successful transactions at unapproved merchants totaling more than $700,000". Removed parents' cards were "not timely deactivated for more than a year". In another case, parents bought books with program cards, "returned the items for store gift cards", and spent those on noneducational items. ABC15 / KJZZ (2026) report continued flagged ESA purchases under the newer platform (headlines only seen in search results, not read) [unverified beyond headlines].

## WHAT IT MEANS FOR PASSPORT

- **Design that fits the rules:** the cardholder is the SGO itself (business cardholder), never the family. The SGO approves a payment request in Passport. Passport mints one virtual card per payment, with an exact-amount limit, the vendor's MCC allowed, and a real-time check that the merchant name/ID matches the verified vendor. The SGO's staff (or Passport's checkout bot, if Stripe allows) enters the card on the vendor's normal checkout or invoice page, and the card is cancelled after capture. The vendor needs no signup, only its existing card acceptance. The vendor sees an ordinary card payment from the SGO; do not hide or disguise Passport's role from the SGO. Passport's fee must appear in the SGO contract. Card funds stay in the SGO's own Issuing balance at the partner bank, and Passport never holds them [inference].
- **Revenue per $1,000 paid to a vendor (one $1,000 online transaction):**
  - Gross interchange, exempt bank, Visa business/purchasing prepaid CNP: 2.65% x $1,000 + $0.10 = **$26.60** (fact 18).
  - Same payment, covered bank (for example Fifth Third): 0.05% x $1,000 + $0.21 + $0.01 = **$0.72**. Not a business.
  - Passport net = s x $26.60 - $0.10 (Stripe card) - fraud/dispute losses, where s is the contract share (unknown, fact 10). Illustration only: s = 25% gives $6.55; s = 50% gives $13.20. Losses are borne by the platform (fact 4), and one $15 dispute erases about 1 to 2 payments of margin.
  - Only if the payment qualifies for large ticket at $1,000 (1.45% + $35 = $49.50 gross) is the gross higher. Qualification rules were not checked [unverified].
  - On Increase, swap $0.10 for $0.25 per card (fact 14).
- **Scale check:** this earns only on the share of scholarship dollars that flow by card at card-accepting vendors. Tuition (the bulk) often goes by ACH or check. So use cards for the long tail (tutors, curriculum, camps, devices) and plan a separate ACH/check rail with no interchange [inference].
- **Next step:** ask Stripe sales and Lithic in writing three things: (1) is SGO-funded scholarship spend on a business card "consumer use"? (2) which issuing bank, and is it under $10B? (3) what share of interchange, and from what volume? Pick the provider on those answers.

## RISKS AND BLOCKERS

- **Stripe "consumer use" ban (fact 11):** spend for a child's education is arguably "personal, family, or household use" even though the money belongs to the SGO. If Stripe says no, it is no. Lithic offers consumer prepaid (llms.txt lists a "Consumer Prepaid Ledger" quick start), which may fit better [inference].
- **Big bank = no revenue (facts 3, 18):** you cannot pick Stripe's bank yourself; get it in writing.
- **25F vendor rule (fact 23):** a vendor that "never signed up" was not "required to reimburse" anything by contract. Card refunds and chargebacks may or may not count. Counsel must answer before scaling to unknown vendors. Vendor verification (fact 24, "pre-approving vendors") is still Passport's job even if the vendor never hears from Passport.
- **Where cards fail:** vendors that take no cards; schools that bill by ACH, check or a tuition portal with high card convenience fees; card minimums or maximums; Stripe's fixed $10,000 per authorization (fact 6), so a $12,000 tuition payment cannot go on one card; force-posts and offline approvals (facts 7, 13).
- **Fraud (fact 25):** MCC blocks alone failed in Arizona. Required controls: SGO staff or the vendor, not the family, holds the card number; one card per approved invoice; exact amount; merchant match; instant cancel; refunds land back with the SGO (fact 8), never as store credit or gift card to a family.
- **Liability and contracts:** the platform carries losses on Custom connected accounts (fact 4). A founder under 18 cannot sign alone (fact 12); a parent-owned entity with an adult officer signs. Providers' KYB will look at the platform's owners as well [unverified, from memory].
- **Licensing:** using a bank-sponsored program where funds sit in the SGO's own Issuing balance likely keeps Passport out of money transmission. That is not confirmed, and the 2-second approval decisions still make Passport part of the payment flow. Get a state-by-state counsel memo before launch (fact 22).
- **Interchange can change:** network rate tables are revised regularly (the Visa PDF is dated 24 Oct 2026), and regulators can change the Durbin cap.
