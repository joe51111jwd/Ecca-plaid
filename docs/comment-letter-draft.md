**Re: Comments on proposed regulations under section 25F, Federal Scholarship Tax Credit (REG-117199-25)**

Dear Sir or Madam:

[COMMENTER NAME AND DESCRIPTION: e.g., "[Name] is a [description of entity] that is building income-verification and giving software for scholarship granting organizations (SGOs)."] We appreciate the opportunity to comment. The Treasury Department and the IRS "request comments on all aspects of the proposed regulations" (preamble, Comments and Public Hearing, 91 FR 62852) and ask "whether there are less burdensome alternatives that adequately mitigate the risk of fraud or improper payment of scholarships under section 25F" (Special Analyses, Regulatory Flexibility Act, 91 FR 62851). We ask for four clarifications that would help SGOs and families apply the rules consistently.

**1. Verification through data the household authorizes (proposed § 1.25F-3(c)(6)(iii)(A)).**

*What the proposed text says.* Under the direct income verification method, "income may be verified through review of written documentation including pay stubs, prior-year Federal or State tax returns, IRS transcripts, Forms W-2, evidence of other income not reflected on such documents (such as evidence of child support and alimony not reported as income) or a certification that there is none, or may be verified through other relevant data sources" (91 FR 62860). The preamble describes the same method differently: an SGO "would verify income through review of written documentation provided by scholarship applicants," and the list ends with "a certification that would verify income through other relevant data sources" (preamble, Part III.F, 91 FR 62827).

*Why it is unclear.* In the regulation text, "other relevant data sources" is a separate alternative to document review, and the certification is a certification that there is no other income. In the preamble, data sources are reached only through a certification within applicant-provided documentation. Neither defines "other relevant data sources" or says whether the household's consent is relevant. SGOs cannot tell whether payroll or bank records that a family itself authorizes to be shared are an acceptable source, or what record of that authorization they should keep.

*Requested clarification.* "For purposes of § 1.25F-3(c)(6)(iii)(A), other relevant data sources include payroll, bank, or similar income records obtained by or for the SGO with the written authorization of a member of the student's household, provided the SGO retains a record of the source, the information obtained, the authorization, and the date obtained." We also ask that the final preamble describe the direct method in the same terms as the regulation text.

**2. Platform and payment-processing fees, and payments collected through a platform (proposed §§ 1.25F-1(a)(18) and 1.25F-3(b)(2)).**

*What the proposed text says.* The section 25F segregated account is "the separate account exclusively for qualified contributions and the earnings therefrom" (§ 1.25F-1(a)(18), 91 FR 62855). An SGO must be "depositing all qualified contributions into its section 25F segregated account" (§ 1.25F-3(b)(2), 91 FR 62858). The preamble adds that "A contribution designated by a donor as a qualified contribution would need to be deposited in this section 25F segregated account" (Part III.B, 91 FR 62824), and reads section 25F(a) "to limit the section 25F credit to individuals making qualified contributions directly to one or more SGOs, rather than through one or more entities" (Part II.D, 91 FR 62822).

*Why it is unclear.* The proposed regulations do not address fees charged by online giving platforms or card and bank processors, which many charities use. An SGO cannot tell whether a fee it pays a service provider affects the donor's qualified contribution or its deposit obligation, or how a donor's optional payment of a service fee on top of a gift should be treated. The "directly ... rather than through one or more entities" sentence appears in a discussion of partnerships and S corporations, but it leaves open whether a gift charged through a payment platform acting for the SGO is a contribution made "to" the SGO, and when.

*Requested clarification.* "A fee that an SGO pays to a service provider from funds held outside its section 25F segregated account does not reduce the amount of a donor's qualified contribution. A separately stated service fee that a donor pays in addition to a contribution, and does not designate as a qualified contribution, is not part of the qualified contribution and is not required to be deposited in the section 25F segregated account. A contribution charged by a payment processor or platform acting on behalf of the SGO is treated as made to the SGO on the date of the charge." If Treasury reaches a different view on any of these points, a statement of that view would be equally helpful.

**3. Expenses for programs outside the school day (proposed § 1.25F-1(a)(14)).**

*What the proposed text says.* A qualified elementary or secondary education expense is "any expense of an eligible student that is described in section 530(b)(3)(A) and any guidance thereunder" (§ 1.25F-1(a)(14), 91 FR 62855). The preamble states that "an expense is a qualified elementary or secondary education expense only if it satisfies section 530(b)(3)(A), including the applicable connection to a school described in section 530(b)(3)(B)" (Part I, 91 FR 62821).

*Why it is unclear.* Families often seek help paying for tutoring providers, summer programs and after-school programs that are not run by the student's school. SGOs need to know when such an expense has "the applicable connection to a school", whether the student must be enrolled at a school when the expense is incurred, and whether the provider must be affiliated with a school.

*Requested clarification.* "The final regulations will state the criteria under which tutoring, summer, and after-school programs provided by persons other than the student's school are qualified elementary or secondary education expenses, with examples of expenses that do and do not qualify."

**4. The income limit (proposed § 1.25F-3(c)(6)(ii)(A)).**

*What the proposed text says.* For the 300 percent limit, "area median gross income means the income limit determined based on area median gross income and family size in accordance with section 8 of the United States Housing Act of 1937 (Section 8)" (91 FR 62860). The economic analysis states that "the income limit must be calculated as the product of 300 percent of the area median gross income and the HUD family-size multiplier" (Special Analyses, Part I.C.2.i, 91 FR 62839), and the preamble says "the IRS anticipates publishing the applicable limitations by geographic area and family size annually in guidance published in the Internal Revenue Bulletin" (Part III.F, 91 FR 62826).

*Why it is unclear.* The regulation text can be read to mean (A) area median income times the family-size multiplier, as in the economic analysis example, or (B) an income limit HUD itself publishes under Section 8, which could be read as six times HUD's very-low-income (50 percent) limit for the family size. The two readings give different results. Based on our own calculation from HUD's fiscal year 2026 Section 8 income limits, the four-person limit in Starr County, Texas would be $116,100 under reading (A) and $251,100 under reading (B), and the four-person figures differ in 143 of Texas's 254 counties. The proposed text also does not say which HUD publication or fiscal year applies.

*Requested clarification.* "The 300 percent limit for an area and family size equals [three times the HUD area median family income multiplied by the HUD family-size multiplier] [six times the HUD very-low-income limit for that family size], determined from the HUD Section 8 income limits for the most recent HUD fiscal year published before the scholarship application date." (We take no position on which method is correct; we ask only that the final regulations state one, and name the HUD publication and fiscal year.) We also ask that the IRS publish the table of limits by area and family size before SGOs first award scholarships funded by section 25F contributions, and state what SGOs should rely on if the table is not yet available.

We would be glad to answer any questions about these comments. Please contact [name, email, phone].
